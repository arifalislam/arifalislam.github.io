01Overview

A platform that moves people from climate anxiety to collective action.

Common Earth is an education and community platform built around a single conviction: that the climate crisis demands deep, systemic change — and that change starts with people. The design brings together structured learning (courses, workshops, leadership training), peer community (local groups), and ongoing insight content into one coherent web experience. The goal is to make it easy for anyone to find their entry point, build understanding, and move toward meaningful action alongside others.

02The Challenge
Many paths in, one shared destination

Common Earth serves a wide audience — individuals overwhelmed by climate news, professionals seeking leadership tools, educators looking for ready-made workshops, and community organisers building local groups. Each group arrives with different needs, motivations, and levels of prior engagement. Core design challenges included:

  • Welcoming visitors who feel paralysed by the scale of the climate crisis without overwhelming them with information before they have found their footing.
  • Connecting distinct content types — long-form courses, short workshops, community groups, leadership programmes — under a single navigation model that feels simple, not sprawling.
  • Communicating transformational value (mindset shift, agency, systemic thinking) through a digital interface where users often skim and disengage quickly.
  • Supporting both individual self-directed learners and teams or organisations booking group learning, with appropriately different flows for each.
  • Building trust and emotional safety early: climate content often triggers anxiety, so the platform tone and layout needed to feel grounding rather than alarmist.
03The Approach
Start with the person, not the product

Rather than leading with a course catalogue, I shaped the experience around three questions a first-time visitor is likely asking: Why does this matter? What can I do? Where do I begin? Answering those questions clearly — before asking anything of the user — drove the following decisions:

  • Led the homepage with a reframing statement ("we viewed the climate crisis as an opportunity to create a more caring world") to shift emotional tone from dread to possibility.
  • Structured the information architecture around three entry paths — Learn (courses & workshops), Connect (local groups), and Lead (leadership training) — so every visitor can self-select without reading a lot of copy.
  • Used progressive content depth: a concise hook at the section level, with fuller detail one click away, so skimmers and deep readers both get what they need.
  • Applied a warm, earthy visual language (natural imagery, soft greens and neutrals, generous white space) to create a sense of calm and groundedness throughout.
  • Embedded social proof and community signals (group counts, participant stories, facilitator profiles) close to each enrolment call-to-action to reduce hesitation and build confidence.
04Design Thinking Process

Make a complex subject feel approachable and action-ready.

The design process moved from understanding who the platform serves — and what emotional state they arrive in — to structuring content that meets them there, then shaping task flows for learning, community, and leadership. Each step was guided by the belief that clarity and warmth are not opposites.

05Solution & Design Areas

Three connected pathways to systemic change.

ALearn
Courses & workshops

The course and workshop experience is built for discovery as much as enrolment. Flagship programmes like Common Earth Insights and Reframing Climate Change are given room to explain their transformational intent — not just their format and duration — so prospective learners can decide whether the content matches their moment. Workshop booking and leadership training paths follow the same logic: clear scope, honest value, low-friction sign-up.

BConnect
Local groups & community

The local group hub helps people find or start a group in their area, turning individual learning into collective action. The design surfaces the social dimension of Common Earth — shared knowledge, mutual support, community-powered momentum — so the platform feels less like a content library and more like the entry to a living network of people working on the same problem.

CLead
Leadership & organisations

The leadership training and tailored workshop sections serve a different user: professionals, educators, and team leads who want to bring systemic-change thinking into their organisations. These flows are designed for group booking, programme customisation, and facilitator credibility — giving decision-makers the information and confidence they need to commit organisational resources and time.

07The Outcome & Impact

A grounded, action-oriented platform for climate change.

The result is a web experience that meets people where they are emotionally — and gives them a clear, low-barrier path into learning, community, and leadership. Rather than presenting climate action as an obligation, the design positions Common Earth as a supportive space where developing new understanding is the first, most important step. The platform now connects courses, local groups, and professional development into one coherent journey from insight to impact.
