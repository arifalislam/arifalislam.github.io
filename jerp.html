01Overview

A connected workspace for the everyday work of managing people.

JERP HRM brings a broad set of workforce operations into one web experience. The design covers how an organization is structured, how employees and their access are managed, how shifts and time away are planned, and how payroll-related services are handled. The goal is to make these distinct responsibilities feel like parts of one coherent product rather than isolated tools.

02The Challenge
One product, many HR workflows

HR teams move between organizational records, employee permissions, schedules, leave requests, and sensitive pay information. Each area has its own data and decisions, yet users need to move between them without losing context. The design challenge was to give these different tasks a clear structure while maintaining a consistent experience across the wider ERP.

03The Approach
Organize around the work

I shaped the experience around three connected questions: who works here and what can they access; when and where are they working; and how are their pay and employee services managed? This structure ties the module set to real operational goals and gives each area a distinct, easy-to-understand place in the product.

04Design Thinking Process

Make complex HR work easier to follow.

The design approach moves from understanding the work to organizing the information and shaping task-focused screens. Each step supports clarity for both day-to-day employee administration and recurring HR processes.

01

Map the work

Identify the people, responsibilities, and recurring tasks represented in the HRM modules.

02

Structure information

Group related capabilities and make the connections between people, time, and pay easier to understand.

03

Shape workflows

Design task-focused views for administration, scheduling, leave, payroll, and employee services.

04

Create consistency

Use shared layout and interaction patterns so different modules still feel part of JERP.

05

Review the details

Check for clear hierarchy, understandable status, and appropriate care around sensitive information.

05Solution & Module Design

Three connected areas of people operations.

APeople & access
Organization management

The HRM Org and User Permissions areas provide a foundation for representing the organization and managing who can access its tools. Keeping structure and access in the same product helps administrators orient themselves before moving into day-to-day people operations.

BWorkforce planning
Time & attendance

Shift scheduling, the holiday calendar, and leave applications bring work planning and time away into a related set of tasks. Their placement supports a clearer view of how schedules and absences fit into workforce administration.

CPay & services
Payroll & employee services

Payroll and salary processing sit alongside salary advances and provident fund and loan services. Separating these related functions into recognizable areas helps users find the right task while keeping compensation work within the HRM experience.

07The Outcome

A coherent design for a broad HRM scope.

The result is a connected web HRM design covering organizational management, user permissions, scheduling, leave, payroll, salary processing, advances, provident fund, and loans. Rather than relying on unsupported performance claims, this case study focuses on the designed scope and the intent behind its structure: make a diverse set of HR responsibilities easier to navigate as one product.
